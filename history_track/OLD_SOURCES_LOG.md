## Entry 7: "Memento rerum omnium circulum esse"

Sent by Danny on 1 November 2026 (a screenshot from a social-media post).

**Status:** the book and engraver are **not identified**. Two web searches found only a Latin forum thread quoting the
same inscription. The style suggests 17th-century Baroque natural philosophy, in the circle of Fludd and Kircher, but
that is a guess and is not recorded as fact.

**Inscriptions:**
- **Top:** *Memento rerum omnium circulum esse*, "Remember that all things are a circle."
- **Cartouche:** *Panscopium naturae encyclicum, quo mira rerum omnium mundanorum pericyclosis exhibetur*, "An
  all-seeing, circular view of nature, in which the wondrous circling of all worldly things is shown."

**What it shows:** concentric rings, from the outside in:
- the planetary revolutions;
- the Sun and the Moon;
- the seasons and the four qualities (*calidum, humidum, frigidum, siccum*);
- the winds;
- the Earth, with three medallions for the animal, vegetable and mineral kingdoms;
- at the centre, a triangle.

It is the same nested-sphere picture as Welling's plates.

**Periods readable in the photo, checked against modern sidereal values:**

| Body | Plate (as read) | Modern | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moon | 27 d 7 h 43 min | 27 d 7 h 43.2 min | matches |
| Mars | 1 yr 321 d | 1 yr 321.7 d | matches |
| Saturn | 29 yr 161 d | 29 yr 167 d | 6 days short |

So the engraver used real astronomical tables of the time, which were very good for the Moon and Mars and a few days off
for Saturn. The other ring numbers are too small to read reliably in the photo and are not recorded.

**Link to our work:** "all things are a circle" is the cyclic-time picture behind the 13/10 clock and the calendar
cycles (the 19-year Metonic cycle). As before, it is a lens for organising ideas, not evidence for the physics.

catalogues to search are WorldCat, the Herzog August Bibliothek and the Biodiversity Heritage Library.

## Entry 8: Johann Zahn, *Oculus Artificialis Teledioptricus*

**The book:**
- 1685–86, Würzburg; 2nd enlarged edition, Nuremberg 1702.
- Sent by Danny on 1 November 2026: about 20 pages from a 35-image social-media post.
- Identified by its structure: *Fundamentum I Physicum seu Naturale*, *Fundamentum II Mathematico-Dioptricum*,
  *Syntagma I–II*.
- Zahn (1641–1707) wrote the first book devoted entirely to the telescope. It includes the earliest description of a
  portable camera obscura.
- **Entry 7 (Panscopium) came in the same post,** so it may be from Zahn's *Specula physico-mathematico-historica*
  (1696, volume I: astronomy). Not confirmed.

### The pages compared with modern optics

| Page (as numbered in the book) | What Zahn says | Modern verdict |
|---|---|---|
| p. 30, Fig. III | Rays refract at the cornea, the aqueous humour and the lens, and form an **inverted** image on the retina | **Correct.** This is Kepler's theory (1604) |
| Synt. II c. II, Prop. IV–V | Every point of a light source sends light in all directions, in a sphere; rays from two points form a "direct cone" and an "inverted cone" | **Correct:** geometric optics, point-source spreading |
| p. 97, Prop. I–II | A small hole in a dark room gives an inverted image; the image grows as the screen moves away | **Correct:** the camera obscura, with image size proportional to distance |
| p. 100, Prop. VI | Light through a hole is "perfect" in the middle and "imperfect" at the edge | **Correct in substance:** umbra and penumbra |
| p. 101, Prop. VII | **Three lights through one hole do not mix**; each lands in its own place, and covering one removes only its spot | **Correct, and deep:** linear superposition. Light beams pass through each other unchanged |
| p. 74 | Motion "determination" split into lateral and downward parts; reflection | Correct as vector components (Descartes' method) |
| p. 106 (quoting Regius) | Colour = light's "aethereal globules" spinning in different proportions; prism order red, yellow, green, blue | **Order right, cause wrong.** Descartes' rotating-globule theory. Newton (1672) showed white light is a mixture that the prism separates |
| p. 111, colour triangle | Five simple colours (white, yellow, red, blue, black) mixed into composites | **Partly.** An early colour-mixing map (in the tradition of Aguilonius, 1613); treating black and white as colours is pre-Newtonian |
| p. 123 | Prism colours: red at one edge, blue at the other, the colour made by refraction | Order right; mechanism wrong (as above) |
| p. 150 | Mars looks redder at night and paler at dawn because the pupil opens and closes | **Partly.** Pupil size and dark adaptation do change colour perception; the real colour is constant |
| p. 152, Prop. XIX–XX | **Angle of reflection equals angle of incidence**; rough surfaces scatter | **Correct:** the law of reflection and diffuse scattering |
| p. 160 | The image through a hole is inverted: left becomes right, top becomes bottom | **Correct** |
| p. 192 | Both eyes' axes must meet for a sharp view (binocular convergence) | Correct in outline |
| p. 210, dragon plate | Upper panel: Lucretius' "flying images" (*simulacra*); lower panel: rays from every point | **History lesson.** The ancient emission and "film" theory of vision, shown beside the correct ray picture. The verse is Lucretius, *De rerum natura* IV |
| p. 217–219 | Refraction measured with **Kepler's box**; "the sine of the incidence angle is to the sine of the refraction angle" as a fixed ratio for each material | **Correct:** Snell's law (Snell 1621, Descartes 1637), with tables made from it |
| p. 228–242 | Lens definitions; the **focus** (the "burning point") where parallel rays meet; double-convex lenses | **Correct:** the basis of every telescope |

**Summary:** about 80% is correct geometric optics, the part that built telescopes. The colour theory is the pre-Newton
part, the same order of colours with the wrong cause.

### The link to our work (history track, not evidence)

- **Superposition** (p. 101): light beams cross without mixing. That linearity is exactly why a CMB map can be split
  into independent harmonic modes, the Cℓ we fit.
- **The sine law and the focus** (p. 217–242): bending light by geometry is the idea behind **gravitational
  lensing**. One of our data sets, ACT×Planck lensing, measures how mass bends CMB light.
- **Prism colours** (p. 106–123): spreading light into its spectrum is how the CMB was shown to be a near-perfect
  blackbody.
- **The camera obscura** (p. 97–160): Zahn's portable box is the ancestor of every telescope camera, including those
  of ACT, SPT and DESI.
- **Polarisation** is not in Zahn: Bartholin found double refraction in 1669, but polarisation was only understood and named by Malus in 1808. Zahn's optics treats only rays. Our P6 prediction
  (no CMB polarisation rotation) concerns a property of light his book never reached.
## Entry 9: Pythagorean Tetractys and "sacred geometry" slideshow (social media, 21 images; checked 1 November 2026)

Arithmetic checked with `python`:

| Claim | Check | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| The Tetractys: 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10, a triangle of ten points | 10 | **True** (the 4th triangular number; Pythagorean, about 5th century BC) |
| The 3-4-5 triangle: perimeter 12, angles 36.87° and 53.13° | 12; 36.87°, 53.13° | **True** (the smallest whole-number right triangle) |
| "Circumference 12 means diameter 12/π" | the definition of π | True, but trivial |
| Triangle with sides π, e and 10^(1/φ), perimeter ≈ 10 | √(π² + e²) = 4.1544 against 10^(1/φ) = 4.1499 (**0.11% off**); perimeter 10.0097 | **Near miss.** Approximate, not a law |
| "Latitude of the Great Pyramid = 29.9792458°", the digits of the speed of light | latitude ≈ 29.97917° (29°58′45″) | Matches to about 4 digits, but the metre (1791/1983) and the degree are human conventions. **Coincidence** |
| 64-tetrahedron grid: 144 triangles × 180° = 25,920 = "years of precession" | 25,920 = 72 × 360, the traditional "Platonic year"; modern precession ≈ 25,772 years | Arithmetic true; the match is to a rounded traditional figure, **0.6% off** the real value |
| "Pythagoras: geometry in the humming of strings, music in the spacing of spheres" | No writings by Pythagoras survive; this wording is a modern paraphrase of the "music of the spheres" idea | **Misattributed** quote |
| Tetractys "represents the space-time geometry of all EM mass-energy" | — | **Not physics** |

**Link to our work:**
- The Tetractys's 10 points are the same count as the Tree's 10 sefirot (our 13/10 clock). It is the triangular way of
  arranging ten.
- The constants-matching slides are a textbook **look-elsewhere effect**. With free choice of units, constants and
  monuments, 3–4 digit matches are expected by chance. That is exactly what our A2 correction measures for the EDE
  angle.
